Higher prices for nonfuel imports and fuel imports drove the advance in September. Prices for U.S. exports increased 2.2 percent in September after rising 1.2 percent the previous month. Imports Import prices advanced 1.1 percent in September following an increase of 0.5 percent in August. Prices for U.S. imports rose 8.1 percent from September 2025 to September 2026, the largest 12-month advance since the index increased 8.8 percent for the year ended July 2022. (See table 1.) Fuel Imports: Prices for import fuel increased 4.8 percent in September following decreases of 0.2 percent in August and 6.8 percent in July. In September, higher prices for petroleum and petroleum products as well as natural gas drove the advance. Import prices for petroleum and petroleum products increased 4.6 percent, and prices for import natural gas rose 1.6 percent. From September 2025 to September 2026, fuel import prices increased 34.5 percent, the price index for import petroleum and petroleum products advanced 35.4 percent, and prices for import natural gas increased 96.5 percent. The over-the-year rise for import natural gas prices was the largest such movement since the index advanced 149.5 percent for the year ended June 2022. All Imports Excluding Fuel: Prices for nonfuel imports increased 0.7 percent in September and have not declined on a monthly basis since August 2025. Higher prices for capital goods; nonfuel industrial supplies and materials; consumer goods, excluding automotives; as well as automotive vehicles, parts and engines drove the increase in September. Nonfuel import prices rose 6.0 percent from September 2025 to September 2026, the largest over-the-year increase since the index advanced 7.2 percent for the 12-month period ended April 2022. Higher prices for capital goods; nonfuel industrial supplies and materials; consumer goods, excluding automotives; foods, feeds, and beverages; and automotive vehicles, parts, and engines drove the 12-month increase for the year ended in September. Foods, Feeds, and Beverages: Import prices for foods, feeds, and beverages were unchanged in September following a decrease of 0.6 percent in August. Higher prices for vegetables, green coffee, and fruit offset lower prices for cane and beet sugar as well as fish and shellfish in September. Nonfuel Industrial Supplies and Materials: Import prices for nonfuel industrial supplies and materials increased 1.4 percent in September after a 1.3-percent advance in August. Higher prices for finished metals shapes and advanced manufacturing; finished nonmetals (boxes, belting, glass, etc.); and agricultural products used for industrial supplies and materials drove the increase in September. Finished Goods: Prices for the major finished goods import categories were up in September. Import capital goods prices rose 0.9 percent for the second consecutive month in September. Higher prices for computers, peripherals, and semiconductors as well as industrial and service machinery more than offset lower prices for scientific and medical machinery. In September, import prices for automotive vehicles, parts, and engines increased 0.3 percent, and the price index for import consumer goods, excluding automotives, rose 0.4 percent over the same period. Exports Prices for U.S. exports increased 2.2 percent in September following an advance of 1.2 percent in August. In September, higher prices for nonagricultural exports and agricultural exports drove the increase. U.S. export prices increased 11.6 percent for the year ended September 2026, the largest over-the-year rise since the index advanced 12.9 percent from July 2021 to July 2022. (See table 2.) Agricultural Exports: Prices for agricultural exports increased 2.6 percent in September, the largest monthly rise since the index advanced 2.8 percent in November 2022. Higher prices for soybeans, corn, and wheat drove the advance in September. Agricultural export prices have not declined on a monthly basis since December 2025. Over the past 12 months, agricultural export prices increased 9.0 percent, as higher prices for soybeans, corn, as well as oilseeds and food oils drove the rise for the year ended in September. All Exports Excluding Agriculture: Nonagricultural export prices increased 2.1 percent in September after rising 1.3 percent in August. Higher prices for nonagricultural industrial supplies and materials; capital goods; consumer goods, excluding automotives; and automotive vehicles, parts, and engines drove the advance. Prices for nonagricultural exports increased 11.8 percent from September 2025 to September 2026. Higher prices for nonagricultural industrial supplies and materials; capital goods; and consumer goods, excluding automotives, drove the 12-month rise. Nonagricultural Industrial Supplies and Materials: Nonagricultural industrial supplies and materials prices advanced 4.0 percent in September after an increase of 2.5 percent the previous month. Higher prices for petroleum and petroleum products; natural gas; and chemicals more than offset lower prices for nonferrous and other metals in September. Finished Goods: Prices for the major finished goods export categories were up in September. Capital goods prices increased 0.5 percent. Higher prices for computers, peripherals, and semiconductors; as well as oil drilling, mining, and construction machinery, and equipment; more than offset lower prices for civilian aircraft, aircraft engines, and parts. Automotive vehicles, parts, and engines prices increased 0.8 percent in September and prices for consumer goods, excluding automotives, rose 1.1 percent over the same period. More information for the major import and export price indexes can be found at www.bls.gov/web/ximpim/largest.htm. Measures of Import and Export Prices by Locality Imports by Locality of Origin: Import prices from China increased 0.7 percent in September, the largest monthly advance since December 2021. Over the past 12 months, the price index for imports from China increased 3.1 percent. Prices for imports from Japan increased 0.6 percent in September following an advance of 0.2 percent in August and a decline of 0.1 percent in July. The price index for imports from Japan increased 1.1 percent from September 2025 to September 2026. The price index for imports from the European Union increased 0.3 percent in September, and import prices from Mexico rose 0.6 percent over the same period. In September, prices for imports from Canada increased 1.3 percent. (See table 7.) Exports by Locality of Destination: Prices for exports to China advanced 2.7 percent in September, the largest monthly rise since the index increased 5.0 percent in March 2022. The price index for exports to China increased 7.5 percent over the past 12 months. Prices for exports to Japan increased 1.7 percent in September following decreases of 0.5 percent in August and 0.4 percent in July. The price index for exports to Japan increased 6.1 percent over the past 12 months. The price index for exports to the European Union increased 3.9 percent in September, and export prices to Mexico increased 2.5 percent over the same period. Prices for exports to Canada increased 1.0 percent in September. (See table 8.) Terms of Trade Indexes: Terms of trade indexes are based on country, region, or grouping and measure the change in the purchasing power of exports relative to imports. The U.S. terms of trade index with China increased 2.0 percent in September following a 0.6-percent decline in August. The September rise in the U.S. terms of trade with China was driven by the increase in export prices to China. The index for U.S. terms of trade with Japan advanced 1.1 percent in September after decreasing 0.7 percent in August. The index for U.S. terms of trade with the European Union increased 3.6 percent in September, the largest 1- month advance for this index since percent changes were first published on a monthly basis in January 2018. In September, the U.S. terms of trade index with Canada fell 0.3 percent, and the U.S. terms of trade index with Mexico increased 1.9 percent. (See table 9.) Import and Export Services Imports: Import air passenger fares increased 12.6 percent in September after decreases of 11.2 percent in August and 11.0 percent in July. Higher prices for European and Asian fares in September more than offset lower prices for Latin American/Caribbean fares. Import air passenger fares advanced 11.8 percent for the year ended September 2026. Prices for import air freight ticked up 0.1 percent in September after rising 2.2 percent the previous month. Higher Asian air freight prices more than offset lower European air freight prices in September. From September 2025 to September 2026, import air freight prices advanced 25.7 percent. (See table 10.) Exports: Export air passenger fares decreased 1.7 percent in September following a 4.3-percent decline the previous month. Lower prices for Asian and Latin American/Caribbean fares more than offset higher prices for European fares in September. Despite the recent monthly declines, the price index for export air passenger fares increased 5.2 percent from September 2025 to September 2026. Export air freight prices fell 2.2 percent in September following an advance of 2.5 percent in August. Lower prices for European and Asian air freight drove the decrease in September. Over the past year, prices for export air freight increased 18.8 percent, the largest over-the-year rise since September 2022. Dash = Not available TECHNICAL NOTE Import and Export Goods and Services Price Indexes - All indexes use a modified Laspeyres formula and are not seasonally adjusted. Price indexes are reweighted annually, with a 2-year lag in the weights. Published series use a base year of 2000=100 where possible. More detailed index series and additional information may be obtained at www.bls.gov/mxp or by calling (202) 691-7101. Merchandise Goods Classification Systems - The merchandise price indexes are published using three classification systems. Items are classified by end use according to the Bureau of Economic Analysis Classification System, by industry according to the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS), and by product category according to the Harmonized System (HS). While classification by end use and product category are self-explanatory, some notes are in order for classifying items by industry. In the NAICS imports and exports tables, items are classified by output industry, not input industry. As an example, NAICS import index 326 (plastics and rubber products manufacturing) includes outputs such as manufactured plastic rather than inputs such as petroleum. The NAICS classification structure also matches the classification system used by the Producer Price Index (PPI) to produce the NAICS primary products indexes. Import Price Goods Indexes - Items are classified by the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTS). Import prices are based on U.S. dollar prices paid by the U.S. importer. The prices are generally either "free on board" (f.o.b.) foreign port or "cost, insurance, and freight" (c.i.f.) U.S. port transaction prices, depending on the practices of the individual industry. Export Price Goods Indexes - Items are classified by the Harmonized Schedule B classification system of the U.S. Bureau of the Census. The prices used are generally either "free alongside ship" (f.a.s.) factory or "free on board" (f.o.b.) transaction prices, depending on the practices of the individual industry. Services Price Indexes - Indexes for import and export air passenger fares calculate changes in the average revenue received per passenger by foreign carriers from U.S. residents and by U.S. carriers from foreign residents, respectively. The air freight indexes are calculated from data collected directly from airlines. These data exclude mail and passenger baggage. The scope of the service being priced is the movement of freight from airport to airport only and does not include any ground transportation or port service. The air freight indexes are presented using two definitions: balance of payments (which represents transactions between U.S. and foreign residents) and international (which represents transactions inbound to and outbound from the United States.) Import Price Indexes by Locality of Origin - Prices used in these indexes are a subset of the data collected for the import price indexes. The indexes are specific to a country, region, or grouping and, beginning with January 2002, are based on the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS) covering goods-producing industries. Nonmanufactured goods are defined as NAICS 11 and 21, and manufactured goods are defined as NAICS 31-33. Export Price Indexes by Locality of Destination - Prices used in these indexes are a subset of the data collected for the export price indexes. The indexes are specific to a country, region, or grouping and are based on the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS) covering goods-producing industries. Nonmanufactured goods are defined as NAICS 11 and 21, and manufactured goods are defined as NAICS 31-33. Terms of Trade Indexes - Terms of trade indexes measure the relative price of exports in terms of import prices for a specific country, region, or grouping. The indexes are calculated as one country, region, or grouping's all-export goods price index divided by the corresponding all-import goods price index on a scale of 100. Relative Importance - A relative importance is a specific index's price-updated value share (expressed as a percentage) of overall imports or exports at a specific point in time. Relative importance values are affected by the trade weights at the point indexes are reweighted and index changes relative to other indexes since the reweight point. If an index is rising in value relative to other indexes over time, the relative importance will increase as well. Revision Policy - To reflect the availability of late reports and corrections by respondents, monthly data may be revised in each of the 3 months after original publication. After 3 months, no further data revisions take place. For example, data first published in the January release will be subject to revision in the releases for February, March, and April. Rounding Policy - Index values are rounded to the tenth decimal place after being calculated. All percent changes are then derived from the rounded index values and subsequently rounded to the tenth decimal place. Uses of the Data - The primary use of the indexes is to deflate trade statistics, notably the foreign trade sector of the National Income and Product Accounts (NIPA) constructed by the Department of Commerce. Other published indexes are useful for general market analysis. For trade in international services, balance of payments indexes are used for deflating NIPA, while international indexes are more appropriate for market analysis.
