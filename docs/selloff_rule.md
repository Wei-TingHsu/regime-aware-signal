# Severe sell-off rule (SS-1): what the old data says in detail

Rule: a stock of the list closes 5% or more below the previous close, on volume above 1.98 times its 60-session average, with a fall larger than 2.72 times its own usual daily move. Old data: 7026 one-day sell-offs of 104 companies, 2005 to 2026; 2013 of them meet the rule (104 companies). Each day counts once ('days'). **This page DESCRIBES the rule on data the search already used; it is not a new test.** The test is the forward record (docs/selloff_forward.md).

## 4. Can it be acted on at the next open? (2018-2026, severe sell-offs, against the S&P 500 over the same span, before cost)

| span | average, basis points | share of days below zero | days |
|---|---|---|---|
| close of the sell-off day -> next open (cannot be traded: it happens overnight) | +3 | 51% | 572 |
| next open -> close two sessions after the sell-off | -18 | 53% | 572 |
| next open -> close three sessions after | -35 | 55% | 572 |
| close -> close two sessions after (what the search measured) | -16 | 53% | 572 |

A cost of about 30 basis points for a round trip is not taken off. If the 'next open' rows are near zero, the fall after a severe sell-off happens overnight and cannot be captured by selling at the open.
