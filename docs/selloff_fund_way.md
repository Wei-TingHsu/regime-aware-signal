# The three sell-off rules without selling short: buying the inverse fund with cash (measurement FD-1)

Registered in TRACK 3.120 before running. Founder's decision, 25 Nov 2026: no short sales -- a margin call would endanger the whole account. Every past trade of SS-2a, SS-2b, SS-2c is redone by BUYING the inverse single-stock fund (the 1x fund first, the 2x fund where there is no 1x) at the open after the sell-off and selling it at the rule's exit, with the account's own US$70,000 and nothing borrowed. Where the rule also buys the S&P 500 fund, the cash is split so both sides carry the same exposure (half and half with a 1x fund) -- so that trade is half the size of the short-sale version. Cost: the founder's fees and the measured spreads (half the bid-ask gap: 1x fund 7.8 basis points in and 5.2 out; 2x fund 11.4 and 6.6; stock 3.6 and 0.6). Each day counts once. A rule PASSES the fund way when it has at least 20 separate days, a gain on average, and a chance under 0.0167 (0.05 / 3) that the average is zero or less.

## What the fund way changes and what it does not

- No margin call: the most a trade can lose is the money put into the fund. The loss itself is not smaller: when the stock rises 30%, a 1x inverse fund falls about 30%.
- An inverse fund resets every day; over two or three sessions its result differs a little from minus the stock's move (the table uses the fund's own prices, so this is counted).
- The funds are young (most 1x inverse funds started between 2022 and 2025): the history is short and comes from one kind of market.
